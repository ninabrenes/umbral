# the mycelial network — integration framework

version: 1.0
date: 2026-04-02
created for: mycelium — open-source psychedelic preparation and integration portal
synthesized from: MAPS 6 Domains, EMBARK, ACE, indigenous traditions, polarity model, contemplative traditions, IFS, somatic experiencing, kundalini/chakra systems
status: original framework. not trademarked. open for community use and evolution.

---

## the metaphor

in a forest, individual trees appear separate above ground. below the surface, a vast mycelial network connects them all — sharing nutrients, sending chemical signals, supporting the weakest members, redistributing resources from the strong to those in need. no tree thrives alone. the network is the intelligence.

psychedelic integration works the same way. your body, your mind, your relationships, your values, your connection to nature, your sense of meaning — these are not separate containers to fill. they are nodes in a living network. when one shifts, all shift. when one is neglected, all suffer. the work of integration is not checking boxes in isolated domains. it is tending the network.

---

## the six nodes

each node is a concentration of integration work. like trees in a forest, each has its own character, but none exists without the others.

---

### GROUND — body, nervous system, physical resilience

> "the body keeps the score." — bessel van der kolk

**what it covers:**
the physical container that holds the experience. sleep, nutrition, exercise, breathwork, somatic awareness, nervous system regulation, substance interactions, medical awareness. your body is the first thing that needs to be prepared and the first thing that needs care after.

**dominant polarity:** masculine (discipline, structure, daily practice)

**optional chakra layer:** muladhara (root) — safety, stability, the right to be here, the right to have a body.

**polyvagal bridge:** GROUND practices work with vagal tone — shifting from dorsal vagal (freeze/shutdown) through sympathetic (activation) to ventral vagal (safe social engagement). the vagus nerve runs through the same pathway traditionally described for kundalini energy movement.

**preparation practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| sleep optimization | 7-9 hours for several nights before, not just the night before. circadian rhythm alignment. | huberman: [sleep toolkit](https://www.hubermanlab.com/newsletter/toolkit-for-sleep) |
| dietary preparation | clean eating 1-2 weeks before. no alcohol. reduce caffeine. light or fasted on session day (nausea management). | ferriss 4-week protocol |
| daily breathwork | physiological sigh (fastest real-time calming), box breathing (4-4-4-4), nadi shodhana (alternate nostril — balances sympathetic/parasympathetic) | [physiological sigh tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=huberman+physiological+sigh) |
| exercise routine | regular movement builds emotional resilience. no intense exercise morning of session. | general consensus |
| medical review | medication check (lithium = absolute contraindication, SSRIs = requires supervision), cardiovascular health, psychiatric history | [UCSF contraindications](https://psychedelics.ucsf.edu/blog/medical-contraindications-to-classic-psychedelic-use) |

**integration practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| somatic processing | trembling, shaking, crying during/after session = body completing frozen trauma responses, not side effects. allow them. | levine: *waking the tiger* |
| gentle movement | walking, yoga, stretching — no intense training for 3-7 days post-session | general consensus |
| sleep protection | quality sleep during the 24-72hr plasticity window is when new neural connections consolidate | huberman: BDNF + sleep |
| no substances | no alcohol, cannabis, or other substances for at least 1 week post-session. protect the plasticity window. | ferriss, huberman |
| body scan practice | daily body scan meditation to notice what the body is holding | [body scan guided](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=tara+brach+body+scan+meditation) |

**journal prompts (GROUND):**
1. what is your body holding right now?
2. where in your body do you feel the residue of the experience?
3. what does your body need today?
4. what physical sensations keep returning since the experience?
5. when do you feel most grounded? what makes that possible?
6. what habits is your body asking you to change?

**journal prompts (GROUND) — ES:**
1. que esta sosteniendo tu cuerpo ahora mismo?
2. donde en tu cuerpo sientes el residuo de la experiencia?
3. que necesita tu cuerpo hoy?
4. que sensaciones fisicas siguen regresando desde la experiencia?
5. cuando te sientes mas enraizado/a? que lo hace posible?
6. que habitos te esta pidiendo tu cuerpo que cambies?

### ROOTS — nature, ecology, earth connection

> "look deep into nature, and then you will understand everything better." — albert einstein

**what it covers:**
your relationship with the natural world. ecological awareness, biophilia, the felt sense of interconnection that psychedelic experiences produce. nature as teacher, not backdrop. the land, the water, the sky, the soil.

**dominant polarity:** feminine (receptive, relational, unhurried)

**optional chakra layer:** muladhara + svadhisthana (root + sacral) — grounding in the earth, flowing with natural rhythms.

**research basis:** lyons & carhart-harris (2018) found that nature-relatedness is one of the most commonly reported lasting changes after psilocybin experiences. the MAPS 6 Domains and THRIVE model both include nature as a distinct integration domain.

**preparation practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| nature immersion | daily walks in nature (minimum 20 min). forest bathing (shinrin-yoku). sit with trees. | [nature-relatedness + psilocybin](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29442493/) |
| nature journaling | observe and record what you notice outdoors. seasons, light, sounds, textures. | practice-based |
| reduce screens | 1 week before: reduce screen time, especially before sleep | huberman |
| connect with land | visit the location where your experience will happen beforehand if possible | retreat center practice |
| sensory awareness | barefoot walks, listening meditation outdoors, feeling wind/sun/rain | contemplative traditions |

**integration practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| forest bathing | unhurried time in forest. no phone. no destination. let the trees hold your process. | [shinrin-yoku research](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5580555/) |
| water time | sit near water (river, ocean, rain). water is associated with the feminine, the unconscious, emotional flow. | indigenous traditions |
| gardening | hands in soil. tending growth. patience. cycles of planting and harvest. | practice-based |
| ecological reciprocity | what can you give back to the land that gave you this experience? clean a trail, plant a tree, reduce consumption. | chacruna reciprocity principles |
| sunrise/sunset practice | bookend your days with sky. no screens. just witnessing the light change. | contemplative traditions |

**journal prompts (ROOTS):**
1. what did the natural world show you during the experience?
2. where do you feel most connected to the earth?
3. how has your relationship with nature changed since the experience?
4. what in nature mirrors what you're going through right now?
5. if a tree could speak about your process, what would it say?
6. what does the earth need from you?

**journal prompts (ROOTS) — ES:**
1. que te mostro el mundo natural durante la experiencia?
2. donde te sientes mas conectado/a con la tierra?
3. como ha cambiado tu relacion con la naturaleza desde la experiencia?
4. que en la naturaleza refleja lo que estas viviendo ahora?
5. si un arbol pudiera hablar de tu proceso, que diria?
6. que necesita la tierra de ti?

### SPORE — inner self, shadow, parts work

> "until you make the unconscious conscious, it will direct your life and you will call it fate." — carl jung

**what it covers:**
psychological insight. defense mechanisms. ego dissolution processing. shadow material (jung). parts work (IFS). self-awareness. mindfulness. creative expression. the inner landscape that the psychedelic experience reveals.

**dominant polarity:** feminine (witness, receive, feel)

**optional chakra layer:** svadhisthana + ajna (sacral + third eye) — emotional depth, creative expression, inner vision, intuitive knowing.

**research basis:** carhart-harris REBUS model — psychedelics relax top-down predictions, allowing unconscious material to surface. schwartz IFS — psychedelic encounters with "parts" (inner child, inner critic, protectors) map directly to the IFS framework. conti's structure/function of self model — defense mechanisms bypass, unconscious accessed directly.

**preparation practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| daily meditation | MBSR foundation. 20-30 min/day. non-judgmental present-moment awareness. start 2-4 weeks before. | [MBSR guided programs](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=jon+kabat+zinn+mbsr+guided+meditation) |
| intention journaling | not rigid goals. "what questions am I sitting with?" hold loosely. | JHU preparation protocol |
| parts mapping | light IFS intro: "which parts of me are afraid? which are excited? which want to control?" | schwartz: *no bad parts* |
| creative expression | draw, paint, write, play music — non-verbal channels for what's emerging pre-session | practice-based |
| fear inventory | name your fears explicitly. "I'm afraid of losing control." "I'm afraid of what I'll see." naming reduces their power. | ferriss preparation |

**integration practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| RAIN method | Recognize → Allow → Investigate → Nurture. the core feminine integration practice. | [tara brach RAIN](https://www.tarabrach.com/rain/) |
| raw journaling | write everything. no editing. no analyzing. no making it pretty. the day after the experience, write 10-20 pages if needed. | ferriss integration |
| IFS parts dialogue | "what does this part need? what is it protecting me from? what would it look like if this part felt safe?" | [IFS institute](https://ifs-institute.com/) |
| creative processing | art, music, movement as non-verbal processing channels. what can't be spoken can often be painted, sung, or danced. | MAPS integration |
| track-by-track journaling | re-listen to session music. pause after each track. write what comes up. powerful for recovering insights that seemed to fade. | kaelen hidden therapist research |
| meditation continuity | don't stop meditating after the experience. the practice that prepared you IS the integration practice. | kornfield, kabat-zinn |

**journal prompts (SPORE):**
1. what part of you showed up during the experience?
2. what did you see that you've been avoiding?
3. if the experience had a message, what was it?
4. what feels different about how you see yourself?
5. what defense mechanism did the experience bypass? what did you see underneath?
6. what part of you needs compassion right now?
7. what is your inner critic saying about the experience? and what would your wisest self say instead?
8. draw or describe the most vivid image from the experience.

**journal prompts (SPORE) — ES:**
1. que parte de ti aparecio durante la experiencia?
2. que viste que has estado evitando?
3. si la experiencia tuviera un mensaje, cual seria?
4. que se siente diferente en como te ves a ti mismo/a?
5. que mecanismo de defensa traspaso la experiencia? que viste debajo?
6. que parte de ti necesita compasion ahora mismo?
7. que dice tu critico interno sobre la experiencia? y que diria tu yo mas sabio?
8. dibuja o describe la imagen mas vivida de la experiencia.

### WEAVE — relationships, community, service

> "we are not human beings having a spiritual experience. we are spiritual beings having a human experience." — pierre teilhard de chardin

> "the only way to complete the journey is to bring the gift back." — joseph campbell

**what it covers:**
how the experience affects your relationships. attachment patterns. communication. community. peer support. integration circles. the people who hold you. and service — turning insight into action for others. karma yoga. the return with the elixir.

**dominant polarity:** both (receiving support + actively giving)

**optional chakra layer:** anahata + vishuddha (heart + throat) — love, compassion, connection, authentic expression, speaking truth.

**research basis:** ram dass (loving awareness, service as integration). MAPS relationships domain. EMBARK relational domain (dual: therapeutic relationship + broader patterns). salzberg (lovingkindness). campbell (the hero must return with the elixir or the journey is incomplete).

**preparation practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| support network | identify 1-3 people who will support you before, during, and after. tell them what you need. | ferriss, JHU |
| the conversation | talk to your partner/close person about what you're doing and why. honest, vulnerable. | practice-based |
| who NOT to tell | some people will not understand. protect your process by being selective. | ferriss |
| arrange integration support | book a therapist, find an integration circle, identify a trusted friend for post-session processing. | [psychedelic support directory](https://psychedelic.support/) |
| lovingkindness practice | begin metta meditation toward yourself and others. 10 min/day. | [sharon salzberg guided metta](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=sharon+salzberg+lovingkindness+meditation) |

**integration practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| sharing with trusted people | tell your experience to 1-2 people who can hold space. not everyone. not social media. | ferriss, zendo principles |
| integration circles | group settings where people process their experiences together. peer support, not therapy. | [psychedelic support circles](https://psychedelic.support/) |
| relational repair | if the experience revealed relational patterns (boundaries, attachment, communication), begin addressing them. | EMBARK relational domain |
| service / karma yoga | "how could what I learned serve someone else?" even small acts. this is how individual insight becomes collective healing. | ram dass |
| community engagement | find your people. the psychedelic community is growing. integration is not solo. | [fireside project](https://firesideproject.org/), local circles |

**journal prompts (WEAVE):**
1. how do you want to show up differently in your relationships?
2. who do you need to have a conversation with?
3. what would service look like as an expression of what you learned?
4. what relationship pattern did the experience reveal?
5. who held space for you? how did that feel?
6. what does love mean to you now — has it changed?
7. if you could share one thing from this experience with the world, what would it be?

**journal prompts (WEAVE) — ES:**
1. como quieres mostrarte diferente en tus relaciones?
2. con quien necesitas tener una conversacion?
3. como se veria el servicio como expresion de lo que aprendiste?
4. que patron relacional revelo la experiencia?
5. quien sostuvo espacio para ti? como se sintio?
6. que significa el amor para ti ahora — ha cambiado?
7. si pudieras compartir una cosa de esta experiencia con el mundo, que seria?

### FRUIT — values, meaning, committed action

> "between stimulus and response there is a space. in that space is our freedom and our power to choose our response." — viktor frankl

**what it covers:**
values clarification. alignment between what you say matters and how you actually live. behavioral commitments. lifestyle changes. career and life-direction reassessment. the "keeping momentum" problem — insights that fade without action. this is where revelation becomes daily practice.

**dominant polarity:** masculine (commit, act, build, sustain)

**optional chakra layer:** manipura + vishuddha (solar plexus + throat) — personal will, agency, power, speaking truth, living authentically.

**research basis:** ACE connect/embody pillars (values + committed action). ACT psychological flexibility model. EMBARK keeping momentum domain. ferriss (integration = behavioral change). campbell (return with the elixir).

**preparation practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|

| loose intentions | not rigid goals. "I want to understand my relationship with [X]." hold loosely. | JHU protocol |
| willingness inventory | "what am I willing to change? what am I not willing to change — yet?" | ACT acceptance |

**integration practices:**

| practice | description | source link |
|---|---|---|
| identify 2-3 insights | not 20. what are the 2-3 actionable things the experience showed you? | ferriss |
| small changes first | don't quit your job on day 3. start with the smallest meaningful change. build from there. | ferriss: "no major decisions for 2+ weeks" |
| behavioral commitments | "I will [specific action] by [specific date]." tell someone. accountability. | ACT committed action |
| values audit (ongoing) | weekly: "am I living closer to my values this week than last?" | practice-based |

## the process layer: receive, recognize, return

within each node, integration moves through three phases. you can be at different phases in different nodes simultaneously. the process spirals — you may return to RECEIVE in a node you thought you'd completed.

### receive (feminine)

be with what arose. don't analyze. don't fix. don't make meaning yet. feel it in your body. let it be present without needing to understand. this is the RAIN method's first two steps: Recognize and Allow.

**practices:** raw journaling, body scanning, somatic processing, nature immersion, music re-listening, RAIN (R + A), sitting in silence.

**the question:** "what is here?"

### recognize (balance)

name what you see. connect to patterns. begin to understand — not with the intellect alone, but with the whole self. this is where IFS parts dialogue happens. this is where themes emerge from the journal. this is where you start to see how different nodes connect.

**practices:** reflective journaling, IFS parts dialogue, pattern identification, track-by-track music journaling, integration therapy, RAIN (I — Investigate).

**the question:** "what does this mean?"

### return (masculine)

bring it back. commit. act. change something in your daily life. share what you learned. serve. this is campbell's "return with the elixir." without return, the journey is incomplete. insights that stay in the journal but never reach daily life are beautiful but inert.

**practices:** behavioral commitments, values alignment, lifestyle changes, relational repair, service/karma yoga, community sharing, accountability, RAIN (N — Nurture, extended outward).

**the question:** "what will I do with this?"

## the four cornerstones

these are not separate from the framework. they are the soil it grows in. every piece of content, every prompt, every practice is held by these principles.

### 1. trauma-informed

psychedelic experiences surface traumatic material. sometimes intentionally, sometimes without warning. this means:
- all content is written with awareness that the reader may be processing trauma
- prompts invite, never push. "if you feel ready..." not "you must..."
- grounding resources are always accessible (breathwork, crisis numbers)
- difficult experiences are normalized, not pathologized ("difficult is not the same as bad" — zendo)
- the tool consistently redirects to professional support for clinical trauma work

### 2. culturally grounded

the practices in this framework have roots. psilocybin ceremonies are mazatec. ayahuasca is shipibo-conibo. meditation is Buddhist/Hindu. breathwork is yogic. we name these roots. we never present extracted techniques as standalone innovations. cultural context is inseparable from practical content.

specific commitments:
- credit traditions by name
- use accurate terminology (onanya, curandera, velada)
- include indigenous voices in content (with permission and compensation)
- link to [Indigenous Reciprocity Initiative](https://chacruna.net/indigenous-reciprocity-initiative/)
- never use sacred imagery decoratively
- bilingual design honors the Spanish-language proximity to many of these traditions

### 3. ethically rooted

the tool is a companion, never an authority. inspired by zendo's "sitting, not guiding" philosophy:
- no guru dynamic. no single voice of authority. diverse perspectives.
- no gamification. no streaks, levels, or achievements. no dependency creation.
- crisis resources visible on every screen, always.
- consistent redirection to professional support
- open source = transparent = auditable = trustworthy
- informed consent before access, not a checkbox wall

### 4. collectively held

integration is not solo work. the most ancient psychedelic traditions are communal. this means:
- content points toward community (integration circles, peer support, therapist directories)
- service as integration (WEAVE node — karma yoga, "how can what you learned serve others?")
- the tool itself is open source — collectively built, collectively improved
- reciprocity is a practice, not a slogan

## the optional layers

### spiritual layer (toggle)

**default OFF.** the default voice is secular, evidence-based, grounded.

when toggled ON, adds:
- contemplative language alongside scientific language
- sacred framing: the experience as encounter with the numinous, not just neurochemistry
- ceremony acknowledgment: references to ritual, prayer, the sacred space
- additional prompts with spiritual framing
- practices: mantra, devotional meditation, prayer if the user prays
- ram dass and kornfield alongside huberman and carhart-harris

what it does NOT do:
- prescribe a belief system
- favor any tradition over another
- make claims about the supernatural
- replace the evidence base

### energy / chakra layer (toggle)

**default OFF.** the default voice uses neuroscience and somatic language.

when toggled ON, adds:
- chakra mapping for each node (as described above)
- kundalini yoga practices (kriyas, specific pranayama)
- energy body awareness practices
- polyvagal-chakra bridge language (vagus nerve as kundalini pathway)
- additional prompts with energy framing

cultural responsibility:
- acknowledge Hindu/tantric yogic origins explicitly
- note that the 7-chakra Western model is a modern reinterpretation (Woodroffe 1919)
- present as one framework among several, not the "correct" one
- source practices from broader yogic tradition, not from Yogi Bhajan's 3HO lineage (abuse findings)
- cite Christopher Hareesh Wallis on original tantric chakra systems
